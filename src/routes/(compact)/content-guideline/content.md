If you plan to write an article for the Monierate blog, this guide is for you.

It overrides any previous rules where they conflict. All writers, internal and external, must follow it strictly.

At its core, every article must solve a real problem with clear, actionable knowledge. If your article does not solve a problem, there is no need to write it.

## Write for human and AI agent intent, not for the algorithm

Your article must solve a specific problem or answer a question that customers have. Examples include:

- Which app can I use to send money to South Africa?
- How do I buy USDT with a debit card?
- What is cNGN?
- Everything you need to know about the gaming business model
- What is Monierate: a deep dive into what they do and how they make revenue

These are real problems our readers face. We treat our readers as customers because they pay us with their attention.

The problem your article solves must be clear in the title. For example, if a customer wants to know which app to use for sending money to South Africa, a strong title would be: "7 Apps You Can Use to Send Money to South Africa."

The article body must avoid repeating sentences, paragraphs, or subheadings. Don't write, "In this article, you will find apps you can use to send money to South Africa," and later repeat something similar, like, "One of the things I like about apps that send money to South Africa." That is repetitive. Instead, simply write: "One of the things I love about these apps..."

The article must end with a clear solution to the problem. Don't leave readers confused or unsure about the right choice. Close with a short summary, no more than three sentences, of the actionable solution.

For example:

> "You don't need to read another blog post or watch another video; all the apps mentioned here are tested and trusted by users. You can pick any of them. If you want instant settlement to a South African bank account, use X; if you prefer lower fees, use Y."

Finally, end the article with: **"Thank you for reading."** We write for human customers, but even robots appreciate gratitude.

You will mostly get it right if you always think about a real customer and the problem you want to solve for them, from the title and meta description, through the intro, to the main content.

## 1. Mission and core purpose

Monierate content educates Nigerians and Africans to make better financial decisions.

Topics cover FX rates, crypto conversions, loans, financial terms, fintech products, and general finance.

## 2. Brand voice and tone

- Straightforward and helpful.
- Conversational yet professional.
- Neutral and factual.
- No hype, speculation, or promotion unless clearly labelled **[Sponsored]**.
- Empower readers without talking down.

## 3. Article structure and length

- **First sentence:** State the problem and the promised solution in one clear line.
  Example: "Losing money on bad USD/NGN rates hurts. This guide shows how to spot and pick the best option every time."
- **Most articles:** 800 to 2,000 words.
- **Shorter explainers** are allowed if the topic fits (minimum 600 words).
- Use short sentences, with a **maximum of 10 words per sentence**.
- Use a **maximum of 3 sentences per paragraph**.
- Break content with subheadings every 200 to 300 words.

## 4. Writing rules

- Use active voice always.
- Define terms on first use (e.g., "USDT: Tether stablecoin pegged to USD").
- Use simple words over jargon.
- Include real examples from Nigeria and Africa.
- Add pros and cons tables, numbered steps, or quick comparisons.
- Embed Monierate tools and links (e.g., the [rate converter](/converter), provider compare) naturally.
- End with a clear next action and a thank you for reading (e.g., "Check current rates on Monierate now. Thank you for reading.").

## 5. Key formatting

- **Currency:** ₦1,450 or $1 = ₦1,450 (use the ₦ symbol).
- **Percentages:** 2.5% (one decimal).
- **Dates:** 2 Feb 2026.
- **Numbers:** ₦1.5 million (spell out small millions in text).
- **Tables:** Clean and labelled, for comparisons or steps.
- **Bold** key takeaways or steps.

## 6. Content types and examples

### Explainer or how-to (most common)

- **Title:** "How Black Market Rates Work in Nigeria"
- **First line:** "Parallel rates confuse many. This post explains why they exist and how to use them safely."

### Term definitions

- **Title:** "What Is NAFEM? Simple Explanation"
- **First line:** "NAFEM controls official dollar supply. Learn what it means for your transfers."

### Product and fintech reviews

- **Title:** "Breet App: How It Converts Crypto to Naira"
- **First line:** "Crypto-to-naira apps save time. This review covers Breet fees, speed, and risks."
- Disclose partnerships clearly.

### Comparisons

- **Title:** "P2P vs OTC vs Banks: Best USD to NGN Option"
- **First line:** "Wrong channel costs you hundreds. Compare fees and speed across methods here."

### Revenue and business models

- **Title:** "How Fintech Apps Like Yellow Card Make Money"
- **First line:** "Free apps still earn profit. Understand their revenue sources to choose wisely."

Avoid daily news, price predictions, or short market updates. Focus on timeless education.

## 7. Editorial standards

- **Accuracy first:** verify all facts and data.
- **Sources:** hyperlinked.
- **Disclaimers are mandatory:** "This is educational content only. Not financial advice. Rates change fast. Verify directly."
- **Neutrality on providers:** factual comparisons only.
- **Sponsored content:** tag [Sponsored] at the top, with full disclosure.

## 8. SEO and readability tips

- Target long-tail searches, such as "how to avoid bad rates on USDT to naira" or "what is parallel market premium explained".
- Use questions in subheadings.
- Add internal links to Monierate tools.
- Write mobile-first: short paragraphs, with big fonts in mind.

## 9. Workflow reminder

- Submit full drafts with sources.
- Edits focus on sentence length, paragraph size, and first-sentence strength.
- Final approval is required for all pieces.
