## FAQs

1. **Is converting crypto to cash legal in Nigeria?**

Yes. [Nigeria's SEC](https://www.aluko-oyebode.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/ISA-2025-and-NTAA-2025_How-the-New-Tax-Regime-Shapes-SEC-Oversight-of-Virtual-Assets.pdf) regulates digital assets as securities under the 2025 Investments and Securities Act, and individuals can legally buy, hold, and convert crypto.

2. **Is converting crypto to cash legal in Ghana?**

Yes, since the [Virtual Asset Service Providers Act](https://www.bog.gov.gh/virtual-assets/) formally legalized crypto trading, with the Bank of Ghana as primary regulator. The cedi remains the only legal tender, but converting crypto is no longer a legal grey area.

3. **Is converting crypto to cash legal in Kenya?**

Yes. Crypto isn't legal tender, but the Virtual Asset Service Providers Act licenses and regulates the activity, split between the CMA and CBK by function.

4. **Is converting crypto to cash legal in South Africa?**

Yes, and South Africa has the most established framework of the four, with the FSCA actively licensing crypto asset service providers since 2023\.

5. **Is P2P actually cheaper than an automatic platform?**

Rarely, once you account for the spread hidden in the rate and the real risk of dealing with an unverified buyer. It only looks cheaper because the cost isn't labeled as a fee.

6. **Which network should I use for small USDT transfers?**

Solana or TRC20. Both keep the network fee under a dollar, compared to Ethereum's ERC20 network, where fees alone can run \$2 to \$15 or more.

7. **How long does the money actually take to arrive?**

On Divest, typically minutes after the blockchain confirms your transaction. We do a daily average of 60 seconds in transaction time. P2P can run anywhere from fifteen minutes to several hours, depending entirely on how fast the other person pays.

8. **Do I need to pay tax on crypto-to-cash conversions?**

Tax treatment differs by country and continues to evolve across all four markets. Keep records of every conversion, amount, rate, date, and check with a local tax professional for guidance specific to your situation.
